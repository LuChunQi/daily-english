### 🎬 场景 1：新工具上线后团队混乱——复盘会上讨论流程改进

**背景：** 团队刚上线了一个新的 CI/CD 流水线工具，但第一周就出了好几次部署事故。Tech Lead 召集开会复盘。

**A — Rachel (Tech Lead)**
**B — Kevin (Senior Dev)**
**C — Nina (Release Manager)**

---

**A (Rachel):** Thanks for hopping on. I want to do a quick retro on our new CI/CD rollout — not to **point fingers**, but to figure out what broke and **tighten things up**. Clearly, the first week was rough.
> 谢谢大家加入。我想对新 CI/CD 上线做个快速复盘——不是为了追责，而是搞清楚哪里出了问题然后改进。显然第一周不太平。

**B (Kevin):** I'll start. The main issue is that the deployment runbook wasn't **baked in** to the new pipeline. We had people pinging me at midnight asking how to roll back. That should never happen.
> 我先说。主要问题是新的流水线里没有内置部署手册。大家半夜 ping 我问怎么回滚。这不应该发生。

**C (Nina):** **Seconded**. And from the release side, the **sign-off** process is too **tight** — we're getting QA approval at 11 PM for a 2 AM deploy. That's not enough time to react if something goes wrong.
> 附议。从发布角度看，签字确认流程时间太紧了——QA 11点签字，凌晨2点就要部署。如果出问题，反应时间根本不够。

**A (Rachel):** Good point. Going forward, I want a **code freeze** by 3 PM for any same-day deploy, with a **buffer day** the morning after. Kevin, can you draft the runbook and **circulate** it?
> 说得好。以后当天部署的代码下午3点前冻结，第二天上午留作缓冲日。Kevin，你起草 runbook 然后发出来？

**B (Kevin):** On it. But I also want to **push back** a little — the tooling docs are incomplete. We need to **dig in** and make sure the rollback steps actually work before the next release.
> 收到。但我也想提一点——工具文档不完整。我们需要深入检查回滚步骤确实能用，然后再发下一版。

**C (Nina):** Agreed. Let's not let this **drag on** — I'll set a hard deadline: runbook ready by Wednesday, tested by Thursday, and we **reconvene** Friday to confirm we're **in the clear**.
> 同意。别拖——我设一个硬截止日期：周三 runbook 就绪，周四测试完毕，周五再碰一次确认没问题。

**A (Rachel):** Perfect. If we can make sure nothing **slips through the cracks**, we should be good for next week's release.
> 完美。如果确保没有遗漏，下周发布应该没问题。

**📝 本场景使用语块（14个）：**
point fingers / bake in / seconded / sign-off / tight / code freeze / buffer day / circulate / push back / dig in / drag on / reconvene / in the clear / slip through the cracks

### 🎬 场景 2：季度预算削减——两个团队争夺剩余资源

**背景：** 公司宣布 Q3 预算削减 20%，Eng 和 Product 团队需要重新分配一个共享设计师的名额。

**A — Derek (Eng Director)**
**B — Monica (Product Director)**
**C — James (PM，协调者)**

---

**C (James):** Thanks everyone. As you know, we need to reduce spending by 20% this quarter. Derek, your team and Monica's team both share Alex, our lead designer. I want to figure out the best split without **bringing either project to a grinding halt**.
> 谢谢大家。如你们所知，本季度需要削减20%支出。Derek，你的团队和 Monica 的团队共享主力设计师 Alex。我想找出最好的分配方式，不让任何一方的项目停滞。

**A (Derek):** I'm **not trying to downplay** Product's needs, but let me **put things in perspective** — Alex is currently designing the new onboarding flow, which directly impacts user retention. Delaying that has real revenue implications.
> 我不是要淡化 Product 的需求，但我们从大局看——Alex 目前在设计新的 onboarding 流程，直接影响用户留存。延迟这个有实实在在的收入影响。

**B (Monica):** And I understand that. But we're two weeks from launching the marketing page redesign. Pulling Alex off now means there's **not a lot of meat on the bone** left for polish, and we ship something half-baked.
> 我理解。但我们离营销页面改版上线只剩两周。现在把 Alex 抽走意味着没有时间打磨，我们会交出一个半成品。

**C (James):** Can I suggest we don't treat this as a **zero-sum game**? What if we **split the difference** — Alex spends mornings on the onboarding flow and afternoons on the marketing page for the next two weeks?
> 我能建议别把这当成零和博弈吗？如果折中一下——Alex 接下来两周上午做 onboarding，下午做营销页面？

**A (Derek):** That could work, but I want a firm end date. Two weeks, not "two weeks" that becomes two months. **I've seen that movie before.**
> 可以，但我希望有个明确的截止日期。两周，不是那种"两周"变成两个月的。这种套路我见多了。

**B (Monica):** Fair. Two weeks works — the marketing page launches, and then Alex goes full-time to Eng. Let's **reconvene** at the end of week one to make sure we're on track.
> 合理。两周没问题——营销页面上线后，Alex 全职转去 Eng。第一周结束我们碰一次确认进度。

**C (James):** Great. I'll document this and make sure nothing **slips through the cracks** during the transition. Sooner we align, **sooner rather than later** we can move forward.
> 好。我来记录，确保过渡期间没有遗漏。越早对齐，越早推进。

**📝 本场景使用语块（11个）：**
bring to a grinding halt / not trying to downplay / put things in perspective / not a lot of meat on the bone / zero-sum game / split the difference / I've seen that movie before / reconvene / slip through the cracks / sooner rather than later

### 🎬 场景 3：同事邀请你参加周末烹饪课——社交闲聊

**背景：** 周四下午，同事 Jenny 在茶水间邀请你参加周六的团队烹饪课活动。

**A — Jenny**
**B — You**

---

**A (Jenny):** Hey! A few of us are **hitting up** that new cooking studio downtown this Saturday — Mediterranean cuisine, wine pairing, **the works**. You should come!
> 嘿！我们几个人这周六要去市中心那个新开的烹饪工作室——地中海菜、葡萄酒搭配，全套体验。你也来吧！

**B (You):** Oh that sounds amazing. I've been **on a cooking kick** lately actually — trying to learn one new dish every week.
> 哦听起来太棒了。我最近正好在迷做饭——每周学一道新菜。

**A (Jenny):** No way, that's awesome. Last time we went, the chef's paella absolutely **blew everyone away**. Like, I didn't know rice could taste like that.
> 不是吧，太巧了。上次我们去，主厨做的海鲜饭惊艳了所有人。我都不知道米饭能做到那个味道。

**B (You):** Honestly, baking is more my thing — breads, pastries, you know. Stovetop cooking is **not really my cup of tea**, but I'm willing to learn.
> 说实话我更喜欢烘焙——面包、甜点之类的。炉灶炒菜不太是我的菜，但我愿意学。

**A (Jenny):** Fair enough! Oh, and **heads-up** — they **jack up** the price for walk-ins, so we're booking today. **Sooner rather than later**.
> 了解！对了提醒一下——现场报名会涨价，所以我们今天订。越早越好。

**B (You):** Good to know. I'll Venmo you after lunch. Any tips for a first-timer?
> 好的。午饭后 Venmo 转你。第一次去有什么建议吗？

**A (Jenny):** Just bring an appetite. Last time I went on an empty stomach and by the time we finished cooking, I was **getting hammered with** hunger pangs. **Lesson learned the hard way**.
> 带好胃口就行。上次我空着肚子去，等做完菜已经饿得前胸贴后背了。惨痛教训。

**B (You):** Ha! Noted. I'll eat a snack beforehand. Looking forward to it!
> 哈！记住了。我先吃点东西再去。期待！

**📝 本场景使用语块（9个）：**
hit up / the works / on a kick / blow someone away / not my cup of tea / jack up / sooner rather than later / hammered with / lesson learned the hard way
