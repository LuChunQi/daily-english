### 🎬 场景 1：周一早晨，Maya 第一次列席 QBR（工作 · 带人上场）

**背景：** 十月末，试点数据不错。Maggie 让每个团队带一位新人在 QBR 上讲 5 分钟"一线视角"。你把机会给了 Maya。会前 15 分钟，咖啡角。

---

**A (Maya, mentee):** Leo, we've got fifteen minutes before I'm on. Any final advice? I want to make them count.
> Leo，还有十五分钟就轮到我了。最后有什么建议吗？我想把这十五分钟用在刀刃上。

**B (You, Leo):** Two things. First — lead with the numbers. Median delay 9 minutes, three launches, zero stale dashboards. When you lead with data, nobody argues with adjectives.
> 两件事。第一——用数据开场。中位数延迟 9 分钟、三次发布、零过期看板。当你用数据开场，就没有人能拿形容词跟你吵。

**A (Maya):** Draft's ready. Second thing?
> 草稿好了。第二件呢？

**B (You):** When Maggie asks for the ask — and she will — don't dance around it. Say it plain: "We want the pilot extended through November, then a rollout decision on Dec 4."
> 等到 Maggie 问"你到底要什么"——她一定会问——别绕弯子。直说："我们希望试点延长到十一月，12 月 4 日做全量决策。"

**A (Maya):** What's the ask — got it. And if she asks how confident I am?
> "你到底要什么"——懂了。那如果她问我有多大把握呢？

**B (You):** Level with her. Nine minutes is the median, not the promise — worst case was eighteen. That honesty carries a lot more weight coming from someone three months in. Volunteering the range reads better than being caught without it.
> 跟她实话实说。9 分钟是中位数，不是承诺——最坏情况是 18 分钟。这种坦诚从一个入职三个月的人嘴里说出来，分量重得多。主动交代区间，总比被问住了拿不出来好看。

**A (Maya):** One more thing — should I mention last week's config incident in the write-up? It wasn't great timing.
> 还有一件事——上周那次配置事故要不要写进材料？时机不太好。

**B (You):** You told her within the hour, fixed it the same day, and the runbook got two new steps out of it. That's not an embarrassment — that's a footnote, not a headline. Own it in one line.
> 你一小时内就上报了，当天修完，runbook 还多了两步。那不是丢人——那是脚注，不是头条。一句话认领它就行。

**A (Maya):** Okay. Walking in now — we're on the same page, right?
> 好。现在进场了——咱们口径一致，对吧？

**B (You):** Same page, same numbers, same ask. Go get 'em. No cap, you're ready.
> 同一页纸、同一组数字、同一个诉求。去吧。不骗你，你准备好了。

### 🎬 场景 2：周四下午，你休假爬山，Maya 独自救火（工作 · Slack + 归属感）

**背景：** 十一月中旬，你终于休了三天假，正在山顶。Maya 在 Slack 上 ping 你——数据管道又出现饱和迹象，和九月初那次如出一辙（Day 103/106 的剧情）。她第一次独自面对。

---

**A (Maya, via Slack):** Leo — queue's at 74% and climbing, 2 PM. I've traced it back to the new event tracker Growth deployed this morning. I'm not bugging you for fun — you said flag it first, not after, and I'm flagging.
> Leo——队列 74% 还在涨，下午两点。我追溯到 Growth 今早上线的新事件追踪器了。我不是闲着没事来烦你——你说过先亮出来别等事后，我这就是在亮。

**B (You, on a mountain, reading Slack):** Good instincts — you did the right thing pulling me in early. Don't wait for my altitude. Check the runbook section you wrote in October: throttle back the consumer, let the queue drain. You wrote those steps — trust them.
> 直觉很准——这么早把我拉进来，做得对。但别等我从山上下来。翻你十月份写的那节 runbook：把 consumer 降速，让队列排空。那些步骤是你写的——相信它们。

**A (Maya):** Throttling now. Growth is asking in their channel if this means their tracker is "broken." The rumor mill is already warming up.
> 正在降速。Growth 在他们频道里问这是不是意味着他们的追踪器"坏了"。传言机器已经开始预热了。

**B (You):** Get ahead of it — one message, before it turns into a headline. Frame it as a capacity question, not a blame question: "Old pipeline, new traffic pattern. We're rebalancing now, no data loss." Then pin it.
> 抢在它前面——一条消息，别等它变成头条。框定成容量问题，不是追责问题："老管道、新流量形态。我们正在重新均衡，无数据丢失。"然后置顶。

**A (Maya):** Sent and pinned. Queue's draining — 68, 61, 55. We're golden, I think.
> 发了也置顶了。队列在排空——68、61、55。我覺得我们稳了。

**B (You):** See? Six weeks of poking around in staging is paying off when it counts. Write the incident down before dinner — your notes have a way of becoming the team's runbook. That's you now, by the way. That's the pass-it-on part.
> 看到了吧？六周在 staging 里到处戳，关键时刻见效了。晚饭前把这次事故写下来——你的笔记总有一天会变成团队的 runbook。顺便说一句，现在那个人就是你了。这就是"传下去"的部分。

**A (Maya):** Funny you say that — the new intern asked me today how I learned all this. I told her: pull things apart, don't just read the docs. She's drinking from a firehose, poor thing.
> 你这么说有意思——今天新实习生问我是怎么学会这些的。我跟她说：动手拆，别光读文档。她现在正对着消防水管喝水呢，可怜的家伙。

**B (You):** Then you know exactly what to do for her — what someone once did for you. And Maya: that's not on you alone anymore either. You've got backup. Talk Monday. Enjoy the view.
> 那你完全知道该为她做什么——就像当年有人为你做的那样。还有 Maya：这事现在也不再是你一个人的担子了。你有后盾。周一聊。让我好好看风景。

### 🎬 场景 3：十二月末，Maya 的第一封年度总结邮件（工作 → 生活 · 写作预习）

**背景：** 十二月，年末。Maya 用 40 分钟写完了她入职以来第一封年度总结邮件，发给你看。你读完只回了三处修改——第一处在第一段。

---

**A (Maya):** Leo, my first year-end summary is drafted. Be honest — but the kind of honest that doesn't make me cry.
> Leo，我的第一封年终总结写好草稿了。说实话——但别是能让我哭的那种实话。

**B (You):** Deal. First note: your first paragraph is a timeline. Execs won't dig for the point — run the numbers, lead with the result: "Pilot extended twice, zero stale dashboards since September, on-call load down 40%."
> 成交。第一条：你的第一段是时间线。高管不会替你挖重点——把数据算好，用结果开场："试点两次延长、九月以来零过期看板、on-call 负载降 40%。"

**A (Maya):** That's brutal but fair. Second note?
> 残酷但公平。第二条呢？

**B (You):** The near-miss in October. You buried it. Bring it up front as one line: caught at 74%, throttled, drained, documented — end of story. A near-miss you report yourself is a footnote. One that surfaces later is a headline.
> 十月那次险情。你把它埋了。拿到前面来，一句话：74% 时截住、降速、排空、归档——完事。自己上报的险情是脚注。后来才浮出水面的才是头条。

**A (Maya):** Noted. And the third?
> 记下了。第三条呢？

**B (You):** The last line — "Please let me know your thoughts." Too soft. What's the ask? If you want the on-call rotation formalized next year, say that. Maggie respects a person who ends an email with a decision request, not a shrug.
> 最后一行——"请告诉我您的想法。"太软了。你到底要什么？如果你想明年把 on-call 轮值正式定下来，就直说。Maggie 尊重邮件结尾带决策请求的人，而不是带一个耸肩的人。

**A (Maya):** Changed to: "Requesting a decision on formalizing the on-call rotation by Jan 10." Reading the two versions side by side — night and day.
> 改成："请求在 1 月 10 日前对 on-call 轮值正式化做出决策。"两个版本并排读——天壤之别。

**B (You):** That's the whole game. Send it — and Maya? When the next new hire pokes their head in your channel next year, remember what you brought to the table this year: the notes, the runbook, the patience. Somebody once passed it on to you. Your turn comes faster than you think.
> 这就是全部诀窍。发吧——还有 Maya？明年下一个新人探头进你频道的时候，记得你今年带来了什么：笔记、runbook、耐心。曾经有人把这些传给了你。轮到你的那天来得比你想的快。

**A (Maya):** Pass it on. Understood. Enjoy the holidays, Leo — you've earned it.
> 传下去。明白。节日快乐，Leo——这是你应得的。

─────────────────────────

**题 1：新实习生不敢在 code review 里提问，怕显得基础差。**（That's not on you / bug 否定式）
> "Nobody expects an intern to know our code style — that's not on you, that's on the docs that never got written. You won't bug anyone by asking — reviewers only get annoyed by questions with zero context. Show what you tried, what you expected, what happened instead. Ask like that and people will fight over who answers you."

**题 2：健身房教练抱怨会员总说"没效果"就退卡。**（pay off 时间维度）
> "Don't take it personally — most of them quit at week six, right before it starts to pay off. Reframe your pitch: show them the week-8 photos of the ones who stayed. Consistency pays off slower than people want, but it pays off every single time."

**题 3：朋友家老菜谱只有 88 岁的奶奶会做，没人写下来。**（tribal knowledge / pass it on）
> "That recipe is pure tribal knowledge — one person, nothing written down, and no backup plan. Don't ask her to 'write a recipe' — she'll say she doesn't follow one. Instead, cook beside her this weekend, film your hands following hers, and write what you see. Then pass it on: teach it to your cousin at New Year. Recipes survive by being cooked, not framed."
